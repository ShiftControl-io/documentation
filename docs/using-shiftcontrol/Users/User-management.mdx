## Overview

The Users page is the heart of ShiftControl. Every person in your organization has a row here, and that row tells you everything you need to know at a glance: their identity, which directories they belong to, whether they're active, and how many groups and apps they touch.

When you make changes here, they ripple through your entire stack. Activate a user and they get access to every app their groups provide. Deactivate them and access is revoked everywhere. Change their department and their group memberships — and therefore their app access — update automatically.

<ConditionalContent modes={["jc-google", "google-jc"]}>

With both JumpCloud and Google Workspace connected, the Users list shows identity data from both directories in a unified view. Each user row displays directory icons for JumpCloud and Google Workspace side by side — so you can immediately see which directories a user exists in and whether they're synced to both.

</ConditionalContent>

## What You See

Each row in the users table shows:

| Column | What It Tells You |
|--------|------------------|
| **User** | Name, email, and avatar — this is the identity used across all connected systems |
| **Directory** | Icons showing which directories the user exists in |
| **Status** | Active (can authenticate) or Inactive (access suspended). A small calendar marker beside the status means a scheduled change is pending — hover it to see when it runs |
| **Last sign-in** | How recently the person signed in — hover to see the exact timestamp. The surest way to spot dormant accounts |
| **Admin roles** | Which Google admin role and ShiftControl role each person holds, so privileges are visible without opening every record |
| **Groups & Apps** | How many groups they belong to and how many apps they can access |

Use the filters to narrow down what you're looking at. The 2SV, last sign-in, Google admin role, and ShiftControl role filters live together in one **Filters** panel with a count badge showing how many are active, alongside the directory and status filters and search:

- **Directory filter** — Show users from a specific directory only
- **Status filter** — Active, Inactive, or all
- **Filters panel** — 2-Step Verification, last sign-in (including **dormant** accounts), Google admin role, and ShiftControl role. Role lists only offer roles someone actually holds, so you never pick an option that matches nobody. A **Reset** button clears the panel's filters without touching your status selection
- **Search** — Find users by name, email, or other attributes

Both admin-role columns and the exact last sign-in timestamp are included when you export the table.

## Common Scenarios

### Auditing access before a compliance review

Your security team needs a list of everyone with active access. Filter to **Active** users, then review the Groups and Apps counts. Users with unusually high app counts warrant a closer look — click into them to see their full permissions. You can export this data for your compliance documentation.

### Finding inactive users who still have app access

Filter by **Inactive** status. If any inactive user still shows a non-zero Apps count, their deprovisioning didn't complete fully. Click in to investigate and revoke remaining access. This is a common finding during security audits.

### Hunting dormant accounts

Open the **Filters** panel and filter **Last sign-in** to dormant accounts — people who haven't signed in within the dormancy window. Sort or export the list, then work down it: suspend, archive, or confirm the account is still needed. The **Super Admin** and ShiftControl-role filters in the same panel let you run the complementary check — everyone holding elevated access — in one pass.

### Bulk onboarding a new team

Your company acquired a five-person design team. Add each user and assign them to the Design group — their app access (Figma, Slack, Google Workspace) provisions automatically based on group membership. No need to configure apps one at a time.

## User Actions

Click the **⋮ menu** on any user row for quick actions.

<ConditionalContent modes={["jc-google", "google-jc"]}>

When both directories are connected, the context menu shows a **directory selector** first — choose **JumpCloud** or **Google Workspace**, then see the actions available for that directory. This lets you manage each directory identity independently.

**JumpCloud actions** (shown for users in JumpCloud):
- **Edit** — Open the user's JumpCloud profile for editing
- **Send activation instructions** — Send setup email with login credentials and MFA instructions
- **Set password** — Manually assign a password
- **Schedule deactivation** — Set a future date to revoke access
- **Disable** — Immediately suspend the user's JumpCloud account
- **Delete** — Permanently remove the user from JumpCloud

**Google Workspace actions** (shown for users in Google, varies by user status):
- **Suspend** / **Restore** — Suspend or restore a Google Workspace account (active users can be suspended; suspended/archived users can be restored)
- **Reset password** / **Send password reset** — Reset or email a password reset link
- **Send sign-in instructions** — Send login instructions (shown for users who haven't logged in yet)
- **Sign out all sessions** — Force sign-out from all Google sessions
- **Change organizational unit** — Move the user to a different Google OU
- **Archive** — Archive the Google Workspace account (preserves data, revokes access)
- **Delete** — Permanently delete the Google Workspace account

</ConditionalContent>

Actions are context-sensitive — only actions that apply to the user's current status and your permissions are shown. For example, you won't see "Suspend" on an already-suspended user, or "Delete" if you don't have delete permissions.

### Add to group and Assign to app

Every row's menu also offers **Add to group** and **Assign to app**, and the same two actions appear in the bulk menu when you select several people — so granting access doesn't require opening each person's record. The related row actions live on the other lists too: **Groups** rows offer **Add users**, and **Apps** rows offer **Assign users** and **Assign groups**.

- **Additive only.** These actions add; they never remove. Taking someone out still happens on the group or app detail screen, where the full membership is in front of you.
- **Several targets at once.** Add three users to two groups in one go. A message confirms what happened, and names any target that couldn't be updated.
- **Directory-aware.** In workspaces running both JumpCloud and Google, each directory's submenu carries its own **Add to group**, filtered to that directory's groups.
- **No accidental duplicates.** The picker shows existing members and assignments greyed out and labelled, so you see current state instead of guessing — and nobody can be picked twice.

### Scheduled activation and deactivation (Google Workspace)

Any Google Workspace user can be scheduled for **suspension** or **activation** — from the row menu or in bulk — so offboardings land on time and people returning from leave come back on the right morning. Pick the date, time, and time zone, and confirm.

- A **calendar marker** appears beside the user's status while the change is pending — hover it to see when it runs, in UTC and your local time.
- **Cancel from the same menu**, right up until the change runs.
- Changes run within a minute of the time you set — not at the next nightly sync.
- Everything downstream still happens: a scheduled suspension follows the same path as a manual one, so offboarding work items, group re-evaluation, and the audit trail behave exactly as they do today. Scheduling an activation on an archived account un-archives it as well.
- Deleting a user quietly cancels their schedule. If Google refuses a scheduled change after retries, your org's Owners and Admins get an email so nothing is silently skipped.

## Bulk Actions

Select multiple users with the checkboxes, then choose an action from the bulk menu. This is how you handle operations at scale — seasonal onboarding, department-wide password resets, or end-of-contract offboarding.

<ConditionalContent modes={["jc-google", "google-jc"]}>

The bulk menu shows a **directory sub-menu** — select **JumpCloud** or **Google Workspace** to apply the action to that directory for all selected users.

**JumpCloud bulk actions:**
Activate, Send activation instructions, Cancel scheduled activation, Schedule deactivation, Cancel scheduled deactivation, Disable, Enable, Send password reset, Force password reset, Set password, Delete

**Google Workspace bulk actions:**
Suspend, Restore, Delete — including the option to delete the Google account entirely when removing several users at once

</ConditionalContent>

<ConditionalContent modes={["jc-only"]}>

Activate, Send activation instructions, Cancel scheduled activation, Schedule deactivation, Cancel scheduled deactivation, Disable, Enable, Send password reset, Force password reset, Set password, Delete

</ConditionalContent>

<ConditionalContent modes={["google-only"]}>

Suspend, Restore, Delete

</ConditionalContent>

Each action is only enabled when at least one selected user is eligible. For example, "Activate" is disabled if all selected users are already active.

## Working with Tables

These behaviors apply to the data grids across ShiftControl — Users, Groups, Apps, Devices, and reports:

- **Export to PDF, Excel, or CSV.** Exports copy the same text you see on screen — readable dates instead of raw timestamps, the MFA method instead of an empty cell, **Active**/**Disabled** instead of status codes, and **Never** for keys with no expiry. Where you can choose columns, export either the visible ones or all of them. PDFs are laid out to be read: landscape pages, wrapped text, and a move up to A3 when a table is too wide for A4.
- **Filenames that say what they are.** Downloads are named for the page and date — like `users-2026-09-25.csv` — instead of `export.csv`.
- **Reset table.** Changed the columns, sort, filters, or page size? A **Reset table** button appears and puts everything back to the defaults.
- **Previous/next follows your list.** The prev/next arrows on a record follow the filtered, searched, and sorted list you opened it from — and the list keeps your filters when you come back.

## Things to Know

- **Changes take effect immediately.** Activating, deactivating, or disabling a user pushes the change to your connected directories in real time. From there, it cascades to every connected application.

<ConditionalContent modes={["jc-google", "google-jc"]}>

- **Dual-directory visibility.** The Directory column shows icons for each directory a user belongs to. If someone is missing from one directory, you'll see only one icon — a quick way to spot sync issues.

</ConditionalContent>

- **Deactivation vs. disabling.** Deactivating suspends access but preserves the account and its data. Disabling is more aggressive — use it when someone needs to be locked out immediately and you don't need to preserve their state.
- **Scheduled actions aren't instant.** A scheduled deactivation means the user keeps full access until that date. Double-check the date if timing is critical for security.
- **Pagination.** The list shows 10 users per page. The total count (e.g., "1 to 10 of 36 users") is always visible at the bottom.
